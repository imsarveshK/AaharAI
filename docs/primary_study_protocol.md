# Primary vegetable quality and cooking study protocol

Official national datasets cannot answer whether the same cultivar loses vitamin C or carotenoids between a farm gate, small town, and metropolitan retailer. This protocol fills that gap without pretending market price or commercial grade is a nutrient test.

## Minimum stratification

Select states across agro-climatic regions and cooking cultures. Within every participating state include:

1. Rural production village or village haat
2. Small town below 50,000 population
3. Mid-sized town of 50,000-299,999
4. Large city of 300,000-999,999
5. Metropolitan market of at least one million

Repeat collection in at least two contrasting seasons. Sample the same species and cultivar wherever possible. Use at least three independently purchased biological replicates per food-market-season cell; laboratory technical replicates do not replace biological replicates.

## Chain-of-custody fields

- Unique sample and container identifiers
- Scientific name, local name, IFCT match, and cultivar
- Farm/source district and retail district using LGD codes
- Harvest, dispatch, market-arrival, purchase, and laboratory timestamps
- Transport mode, packaging, temperature logger identifier, and storage conditions
- Market type and settlement class
- Edible portion, visual defect score, firmness, colour, moisture loss, and Brix where relevant
- Laboratory, method, limit of detection, limit of quantification, replicate, and accreditation status

Unknown timestamps or cultivars must be stored as null, never estimated from distance or appearance.

## Priority analytes

Use food-specific panels rather than an identical expensive panel for every vegetable. Common priorities are moisture, vitamin C, folate, beta-carotene/total carotenoids, selected minerals, total phenolics, pesticide residues, heavy metals, and microbial indicators. Pre-register the analytical method and acceptable holding time for every analyte.

## Cooking experiment

For each culturally representative recipe, weigh every raw ingredient and the complete cooked yield. Record peeling, chopping, soaking, fermentation, cooking time, temperature/pressure, water added, whether liquid is retained, oil uptake, holding, and reheating. Analyse raw and cooked matched composites. Report absolute nutrient mass, concentration per 100 g cooked, true retention, and—where measured—bioaccessibility as distinct outcomes.

## Analysis boundaries

- Compare like cultivar, season, and production source wherever possible.
- Model market/settlement, season, cultivar, harvest age, and temperature exposure separately.
- Do not infer individual health effects by joining market averages to district NFHS outcomes alone.
- Use survey weights and design variables for NFHS/HCES estimates.
- Publish missingness, suppression, measurement uncertainty, and sensitivity analyses.
